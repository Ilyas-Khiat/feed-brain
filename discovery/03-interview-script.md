# Test 3 — Interviews

**Status:** script ready, no external interviews yet.

Tests H4. Start only after Test 1, so you know what you're looking for.

Target: **saturation within one segment** — three consecutive interviews that
teach you nothing new. Usually 8-15. Saturation does not transfer between
segments, so pick one and stay in it.

## Pick one segment first

In rough order of how likely they are to have a budget:

| Segment | Why | Risk |
|---|---|---|
| Newsletter / content people covering AI | Consume heavily, must produce output, forgetting is directly costly | Small, may build their own |
| Consultants & analysts | Paid to sound current | Hard to reach |
| Researchers, grad students | High volume, citation pressure | No money |
| Devs learning from conference talks | Concrete recall needs | Low frequency |
| Casual learners (you) | Largest, cheapest to reach | **Probably no budget** |

Note the pattern in that last row: reachability correlates with not having the
problem badly. The people drowning in it don't take calls.

## Script

**Do not describe your idea.** The moment you do, the interview ends and a sales
call begins. Open with: *"I'm trying to understand how people handle video content
they've watched. I'm not selling anything."*

1. Walk me through the **last** video you watched that you'd call useful. When was it?
2. Have you gone back to anything you watched more than a month ago? Tell me about
   the last time. *(If never — that's your answer about this person.)*
3. Do you keep anything about videos you watch? **Can you show me?**
   → Always ask for the artifact. Rung-3 evidence hides in artifacts, and people
   under-report their own workarounds.
4. What made you start doing that?
5. How long have you kept it up? What happened the last time it lapsed?
6. What did you try before that? What made you stop?
7. Have you ever paid for anything in this area? What happened?
8. **What happens if you just... do nothing about this?**
   → Then stay quiet. The second thing they say is the true one.

Ask about the last specific time, never about what they generally do. Memory of
specifics is honest; generalization is theory.

## Check before concluding anything

- **Friendly sample** — did I interview whoever was reachable?
- **Wrong segment** — 15 consistent interviews inside a segment that can't buy is 15 wasted interviews.
- **Enthusiastic non-buyer** — loves it, no budget, no authority. Can this person actually pay?
- **Demo effect** — are they reacting to my energy in the room, or would they do this alone at 4pm on a Thursday?
- **Retroactive criteria** — did I decide what counted as success before or after seeing the answers?
