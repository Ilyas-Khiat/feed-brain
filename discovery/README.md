# Discovery

Before writing any code for Feed Brain (first called YouTube Brain), I wrote down what I believed about the problem, what result would prove each belief wrong, and how I'd test it. These notes are that work, kept as written, including the parts that weakened the idea.

## The method

- **[Hypotheses and kill conditions](00-hypotheses.md).** Five claims (forgetting is real, it has a cost, it happens often enough to build a habit, a segment beyond me has it), each with the result that would make it false. The file also has an evidence ladder: a compliment counts for nothing, and only money or repeat use proves demand. There is an exit gate for this first stage too (the problem stage, S0 below), and a log of every goalpost I moved and why.
- **[Recall audit](01-recall-audit.md).** A protocol to measure forgetting instead of guessing at it: 10 videos from 3 to 6 weeks ago, recalled before rewatching, each scored for what losing it actually cost.
- **[Incident log](02-incident-log.md).** Three weeks of logging every moment I wanted something from a video and couldn't get it, and what solved it. If Google or an LLM wins in under a minute, an archive has no job.
- **[Interview script](03-interview-script.md).** For finding out whether anyone else has the problem badly enough to pay: never pitch, ask about the last specific time, and ask to see the workaround.
- **[Evidence log](04-evidence-log.md).** What the first subject (me) showed, scored against the ladder.

## What it changed

The first interview, with myself, moved the problem. I could find the video I'd lost in a few minutes by scrolling my history, so retrieval friction was weaker than I thought (hypothesis H2a got a harder bar, see the goalpost log). The words that stood out were *"I don't have the reflex"* to save it: the failure was at **capture**. That's why the tool asks for one click on a video and none at all on LinkedIn and X, where saving or liking a post already sends it.

It also named a second job I hadn't planned for: using a video as a way into deeper study (papers, code, a conversation). That's what the concept notes and their links are for.

## Where it stands

Honestly: the problem stage isn't passed. The recall audit, the incident log and the interviews are protocols I wrote but haven't run yet, and every finding so far rests on one subject, me. I built Feed Brain as a personal tool first, and its run log is now a second source of evidence:

- **Capture gets used.** 69 captures between 24 September and 10 November 2026: 37 videos, 15 LinkedIn posts and 17 X posts.
- **Asking questions is rare so far.** *Ask the brain* was used 6 times, 4 of them on the day it shipped. That fits the warning in H3: a need that comes up rarely doesn't build a habit.

The next step is the one in [`04-evidence-log.md`](04-evidence-log.md): interviews outside my own head.

*S0 is the first stage of the validation framework these notes follow: is the problem real, for a segment that already spends time or money on it, before anything gets built for others.*
