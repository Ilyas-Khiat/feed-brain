# Test 1 — Retroactive recall audit

**Status:** protocol written, not run yet.

Cost: ~40 min, once. Do this first — it tests H1 and H2 together and turns an
invisible problem into a measurable one.

## Why this instead of "notice when I forget"

You cannot count incidents you never notice. Complete forgetting is silent — it
produces no query and no feeling. This test forces the comparison instead of
waiting around for it to surface on its own.

## Method

1. Open YouTube history, scroll back to **3-6 weeks ago**. Take 10 videos you
   watched most or all of. Take them in order — do not cherry-pick memorable ones.
2. For each, **before reopening it**, write from memory: the main claim, any
   specific fact, and who was talking.
3. Then open it. Score honestly.
4. Fill the consequence column last, and be ruthless. "I'd like to still know it"
   is not a consequence.

## Scoring

**Recall:**
- `2` — could explain the core argument to someone
- `1` — topic and vague gist only
- `0` — nothing, or don't recall watching it

**Consequence:** something concrete that broke, or would have gone differently.
A conversation where it mattered. A decision. Work output. If the honest answer
is none, write `NONE`. **The NONEs are the finding.**

- **Mean recall** out of 2 — H1 false if >= 1.5

## How to read the result

| Recall | Consequence | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| High | — | Premise is wrong. Stop. |
| Low | Few | **Most likely outcome.** You forget things that don't matter. That is not a product. |
| Low | Many | Problem is real for you. Proceed to H3 and H4. |

The middle row is the one to brace for. It does not mean you're wrong about
forgetting — it means forgetting isn't costing you anything, which is a different
and much worse finding for a business.
